FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Practical information about acupuncture, Chinese herbal medicine, appointments, payments and the clinic.

Clinical suitability is individual and should be discussed with an appropriately qualified practitioner.
1. What conditions can be treated with acupuncture?

Patients may seek acupuncture for headaches, back pain, sciatic pain, stress-related symptoms and other presentations. Suitability and evidence vary by condition, so discuss your individual circumstances with the practitioner.

2. Does acupuncture hurt?

The clinic uses fine needles with guide tubes. Sensation varies; some people notice little discomfort while others may feel brief pressure, tingling or aching.

3. Is acupuncture safe?

Acupuncture should be performed by a suitably trained and registered practitioner using appropriate infection-control procedures. Adverse effects can still occur.

4. Does acupuncture have side effects?

Possible effects can include minor bleeding, bruising, soreness, dizziness or other reactions. Seek medical advice for significant or persistent symptoms.

5. How many acupuncture sessions do I need?

There is no fixed number for everyone. The number of sessions depends on the individual condition and response.

6. What should I do before an acupuncture session?

It is generally sensible to avoid arriving very hungry; the clinic advises eating beforehand. Tell the practitioner about relevant medical conditions and medicines.

7. What happens at the first visit?

The practitioner will collect details about your health concern, perform relevant assessment, discuss a treatment plan and, where appropriate, provide treatment.

8. Can Chinese herbal medicine be used for health problems?

Chinese herbal medicine is used for a range of presentations, but whether it is appropriate depends on assessment, possible interactions and your medical history.

9. Do Chinese herbs have side effects?

Yes, adverse effects and interactions can occur. Possible effects can include nausea or an unpleasant taste, but other reactions are also possible.

10. Can children take Chinese herbal medicine?

Children should only receive herbal medicine following assessment by an appropriately qualified practitioner, with age-appropriate products and dosing.

11. How long does Chinese herbal medicine take to work?

This varies according to the person, condition, product and treatment plan.

12. What about pregnancy and Chinese herbs?

Pregnancy requires particular caution. Do not use herbal products in pregnancy without advice from an appropriately qualified practitioner who knows you are pregnant.

13. Are you a registered Chinese medicine practitioner?

Dr Sherman Gu is listed as practising in acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine with AHPRA registration number CMR0001738861. Registration details should be checked periodically against the AHPRA register.

14. Are the cups and needles cleaned?

The clinic states that acupuncture needles are single-use disposable needles and that reusable equipment is cleaned according to applicable infection-control requirements.

15. How long is an acupuncture or cupping appointment?

An initial consultation and treatment is generally around 30–45 minutes, with follow-up visits around 30 minutes; cupping duration may vary.

16. How deep are acupuncture needles inserted?

Depth depends on the anatomical location, technique and patient. This is determined by the practitioner; it is not a fixed depth.

17. Where are you located?

18. What payment methods do you accept?

Cards, cheque and cash are accepted, and HICAPS is available for eligible claims.

19. How much experience does the practitioner have?

Dr Sherman Gu has practised acupuncture and Chinese herbal medicine since 1982.

20. Can I claim through private health insurance?

Eligibility depends on your fund, policy, treatment and provider arrangements. Private health rebates may be available; confirm directly with your insurer before treatment.

21. Do you treat WorkSafe or TAC clients?

WorkSafe or TAC funding may apply where approval has been obtained. Confirm eligibility with the relevant insurer or scheme before treatment.
