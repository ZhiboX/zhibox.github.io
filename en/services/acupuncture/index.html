Chinese Acupuncture

Acupuncture is a branch of Chinese medicine involving the insertion of fine, single-use needles at selected points. Consultation considers the patient’s symptoms and overall presentation before a treatment plan is discussed.

How acupuncture is approached

At Knox Chinese Medicine, acupuncture is discussed within a traditional Chinese medicine framework that considers balance and the body’s overall presentation. A consultation may include questions about sleep, appetite, diet, bowel function and other symptoms, together with traditional observation such as the tongue and pulse.

Clinical outcomes vary. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant, use medicines, or have concerns about whether acupuncture is appropriate, discuss this with an appropriately qualified health practitioner.

Potential areas of use

Patients may seek acupuncture for a range of reasons. The examples below do not represent guarantees of outcome.

1. Pain management

Acupuncture is commonly used as part of pain-management care, including for some musculoskeletal conditions and headaches.

2. Stress and relaxation

Some patients use acupuncture as part of a broader approach to stress management and relaxation.

3. Sleep

Some people report changes in sleep following treatment; individual responses vary.

4. Immune function

Possible effects on immune function are sometimes discussed in relation to acupuncture; clinical relevance depends on the individual condition.

5. Digestive health

Acupuncture may be discussed as part of care for some digestive symptoms.

6. Headache and migraine

Acupuncture is used by some people seeking support for headache and migraine symptoms.

7. Menstrual health

Acupuncture may be discussed for menstrual symptoms depending on the individual presentation.

8. Allergy symptoms

Some patients seek acupuncture for allergic-rhinitis symptoms.

9. Energy and fatigue

Some patients report changes in perceived energy or fatigue after treatment.

10. Holistic assessment

Traditional Chinese medicine consultation considers patterns of symptoms and the person’s overall presentation.

Acupuncture FAQs

How does acupuncture work?

The clinic uses acupuncture within a traditional Chinese medicine framework. Contemporary explanations and evidence vary by condition; your practitioner can discuss the intended rationale for your individual treatment.

Is acupuncture safe?

When provided by a suitably trained and registered practitioner using appropriate infection-control procedures, acupuncture is generally considered a low-risk intervention, although adverse effects can occur.

How long does a session take?

Sessions are generally around 30–60 minutes depending on the consultation and treatment required.

Is acupuncture painful?

The needles are very fine. Some people experience little discomfort, while others may notice brief sensations at insertion or during stimulation.
